Why the score distribution has peaks

The multi-modality is not a property of the model. It is forced by the metric's own arithmetic: five of the six components are discrete, and only one varies continuously.

The mechanism

v1 PDMS is two multiplicative gates times a weighted sum:

PDMS  =  NC × DAC × ( 5·EP  +  5·TTC  +  2·C ) / 12

Reading the per-scene values straight out of the scoring CSV shows that five of the six components are switches — they take one of two or three fixed values and nothing in between. Only ego progress varies smoothly:

ComponentWeightValues it takes, and how oftenBehaviour
no_at_fault_collisions (NC)gate 1 → 97.2% · 0.5 → 0.4% · 0 → 2.5% switch — multiplies the whole score
drivable_area_compliance (DAC)gate 1 → 92.3% · 0 → 7.7% switch — multiplies the whole score
time_to_collision (TTC)5⁄12 1 → 92.6% · 0 → 7.4% switch — adds or removes a fixed 0.417
comfort (C)2⁄12 1 → 100.0% · 0 → 2 scenes switch — effectively always on
ego_progress (EP)5⁄12 median 0.86 · quartiles 0.72–1.00 dial — the only continuous term
driving_direction (DDC)0 1 → 96.1% · 0.5 → 2.5% · 0 → 1.4% computed but inert in v1

Ego progress is itself piled up at the ends — 29.6% of scenes score exactly 1.0 and 10.0% exactly 0 — but the remaining 60% spread continuously across the range, and that is what fills each band.

So the score is a continuous variable sitting on top of a small set of discrete offsets. Ego progress smears each group across a band 5⁄12 = 0.417 wide, and the binary terms shift whole bands up or down by fixed amounts. Each combination becomes its own hump.

The gaps are structural

The empty stretch between roughly 0.583 and 0.663 is not sampling noise — it is arithmetic. A scene with TTC = 0 is capped at (5·1 + 0 + 2)/12 = 0.583, and a scene with TTC = 1 is floored at (0 + 5 + 2)/12 = 0.583. The value 0.583 is a hard boundary between two populations, and scenes only appear just above it when ego progress is also poor — which is rare among scenes that passed everything else.

The same logic explains the emptiness between 0 and 0.326: to land there you would need to pass both gates and still score almost nothing on progress, which essentially does not happen. Scenes that go wrong go wrong through a gate, and land on exactly zero.

What follows from this

  • The mean is a poor summary. An 83.02 aggregate is a mixture of 85.7% of scenes scoring above 0.66 and 10% scoring exactly zero — not a population centred on 0.83.
  • Improving the score means moving scenes between modes, not nudging them. Rescuing one gate failure is worth more than improving progress on dozens of already-passing scenes.
  • Rank statistics on the raw distribution are dominated by the ties. 26.8% of scenes sit at exactly 1.000 and 10.0% at exactly 0 — which is why every correlation in the findings report is also reported over the interior only.
