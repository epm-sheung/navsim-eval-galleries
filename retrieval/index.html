What each DINO thinks “a similar scene” means

Thirty query scenes, each one’s t0 camera frame — the last frame of the past — matched against the t0 frame of all 12,146 navtest scenes. Once with stock DINOv2 ViT-S/14, once with the trunk our driving model fine-tuned. Same image, same preprocessing, same CLS descriptor: only the weights differ.

How it was built

Descriptor is the x_norm_clstoken of forward_features, L2-normalised, compared by cosine. Images come from the converted navtest cache at their stored 504×280, so the transform is ToTensor+Normalize (already at model size) — identical to what the waypoint models saw at eval time. The fine-tuned trunk is the one carried inside waypoint_navsim_ft/epoch013-best.ckpt; it differs from stock in 30 of 175 tensors (blocks 10–11 and the final norm). Queries were chosen from metadata only — three per driving-command × speed-tercile cell, plus the three lowest-PDMS scenes — so neither embedding had a hand in picking them.

Why “other logs only” is the default view. navtest scenes from one log are seconds apart, so the plain top 1 is nearly always the same car a moment later: a correct answer and a useless picture. Stock fills 67% of its raw top-10 that way, the fine-tuned trunk 53%. Switch to Raw top-10 to see it.

Neighbours shared by both models, out of 10: 2.5 across other logs, 5.6 on the raw list — against 0.0082 expected by chance. On the same image the two CLS vectors have cosine 0.127: touching 30 tensors moved the representation to a largely unrelated direction, not a nudge.

The fine-tuned trunk retrieves scenes that match the query’s driving command more often and are closer in speed, which is what a trunk trained through a waypoint head should do. It is a descriptive result on 30 queries, not a benchmark: no retrieval ground truth exists here, so command and speed agreement are stand-ins for “driving-relevant”, and they are exactly the quantities the training signal depended on.
