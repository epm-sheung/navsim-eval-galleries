Green criterion: which distance, which threshold

A retrieved neighbour is “green” when its ground-truth 4 s future is close to the query’s. This page tests four ways of measuring “close” over many thresholds, using every descriptor variant in the benchmark (__NQ__ queries, top-10 out of __NC__ navtest scenes, own log excluded). Both futures are GT, 8 waypoints at 0.5 s, each in its own t0 ego frame.

Result

  • Recommended: speed-latent distance ≤ __REC_THR__ (10% of random scene pairs pass). It separates models with median |z| = __REC_Z__, against __ADE_Z__ for today’s ADE ≤ 2 m (__ADE_CH__% chance).
  • Best ADE threshold: flat between about 1.6 and 3.4 m. Today’s 2 m is already at the optimum, and above about 3.4 m it saturates (the best model reaches 9.75 of 10).
  • The speed latent beats ADE at every chance rate from 2% to 50%, by up to +3.75 in median |z|. The full training loss ties the latent but needs a fitted scale factor between its two terms, so the latent alone is simpler.
  • The model ranking barely changes (Kendall τ ≥ 0.93 against the continuous distance), so earlier conclusions still hold. Under the latent, every model gets about 1–2 more green neighbours.

How the criteria are compared

  • Matched chance rate. The criteria have different units, so each threshold is set to the quantile of the distance between random cross-log scene pairs that gives the same chance rate. Comparing at equal chance rate is the fair test.
  • Separating power = median |z| of the paired per-query difference in green count, over all 80 within-pipeline variant pairs. Higher means real differences between models show up more reliably.
  • Ranking fidelity = Kendall τ between the variant ranking by green count and by the same distance left continuous.
  • Validity = AUC for predicting a rule-based “plausible” label (same manoeuvre: stop / left / right / straight, plus the same speed tertile). The label is derived from the same trajectories and includes speed, which favours the latent, so treat it as a sanity check, not ground truth.
  • Speed latent = L1 distance between the frozen velocity-autoencoder codes of the two whitened velocity sequences, the term our training loss uses. Full loss = 0.7 × latent + 0.3 × __HSCALE__ × Huber on normalised positions (one global scale factor). The autoencoder reconstructs navtest futures with an error of __SAN__ (navtest vs its own training data), so it is valid on navtest.
